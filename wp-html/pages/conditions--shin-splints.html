What you notice

Symptomsand causes

If this sounds like your pain, there is a structural reason for it. The assessment is what finds it.

Common symptoms

  • Aching along the inside edge of the shin
  • Comes on during or after running and eases with rest
  • Tender along a stretch of bone rather than one point
  • Worse on hard surfaces or in worn shoes

What causes it

Shin pain usually reflects how load is being transmitted up from the foot. A sudden increase in training, a change of surface, worn footwear or the way the foot rolls through stance can all concentrate stress along the tibia.

In detail

Understandingshin splints

There are three types of shin splints. Each is named for the part of the shin that hurts, and each is linked to a different muscle and a different way the foot moves.

Three types of shin splints

Foot rolls in

Inner edge (medial)

The arch drops and the foot lengthens, stretching the tibialis posterior muscle until it develops tiny tears.

Rolls out, then in

Front (anterior)

The foot lands on its outer edge, then snaps inwards. The sudden change strains the tibialis anterior muscle at the front.

Foot rolls out

Outer edge (lateral)

The foot stays rolled out through the whole step, straining the peroneal muscles on the outside of the leg.

01

Why it builds with training

The strain is repetitive, so it gets worse as training volume and intensity go up, because the same foot movement repeats with every step.

02

Where it hurts guides the orthotic

An inner-edge type usually needs the rolling-in controlled. Front and outer types need the forefoot corrected so the foot stops landing on its outer edge.

03

What a plan can include

Orthotics, dry needling, shockwave, manipulation and massage.

How we diagnose it

The cause,not the sore spot

Shin Splints is diagnosed with the Najjarine Biomechanical Assessment, so treatment is prescribed against a finding rather than a symptom.

Treatment addresses the mechanics and the training load together, since changing one without the other usually brings it back.
