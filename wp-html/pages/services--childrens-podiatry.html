What it is

Children's Podiatryexplained

Children’s feet are still forming, and some things that look alarming settle on their own while others do not. An assessment tells you which you are looking at, and whether anything needs doing now.

Parents who have noticed in-toeing, out-toeing, flat feet, tripping, or a child who complains of sore legs after activity or at night.

Where it is available
In detail

Understandingchildren's podiatry

Children’s feet and legs are still forming. It is important to assess the child’s biomechanics rather than putting pain down to growing pains.

Common in growing children

Heel, age 8–14

Sever’s disease

Soreness at the back of the heel, worse after running and when getting up. Linked to feet that roll in or out, not only to growth.

Knee, age 13–17

Osgood-Schlatter

A tender lump below the kneecap where the tendon pulls on the growing shin bone.

Any age

In- and out-toeing

Often a twist in the shin bone, inherited from a parent.

01

"Growing out of it"

Where in-toeing comes from a twisted shin, the body often hides it by tightening the hip muscles rather than correcting it. Measuring it is how you know.

02

Who Sever’s affects most

Active, fast-growing children, more often boys, whose feet roll in or out and put repeated stress on the heel growth plate.

03

What a plan can include

Orthotics, heel lifts, foot mobilisation, strapping and calf massage, reviewed as your child grows.

How it works here

Assessment first,then treatment

Children's Podiatry is prescribed against a diagnosis, never against a symptom. That is what the Najjarine Biomechanical Assessment is for.

01

Assess

We assess how your child stands, walks and runs, and compare that against what is normal for their age.

02

Treat

We explain what we found in plain terms, including when the answer is that no treatment is needed yet.

03

Address the cause

If something does need addressing, treatment is staged around growth and reviewed as they develop.
