What you notice

Symptomsand causes

If this sounds like your pain, there is a structural reason for it. The assessment is what finds it.

Common symptoms

  • Arches that flatten when you stand
  • Aching in the arch, heel or inside of the ankle after activity
  • Shoes wearing unevenly on the inside edge
  • Tired legs after standing rather than sharp pain

What causes it

A low arch is a shape, not automatically a problem. It matters when the way the foot rolls through stance puts strain on tissue further up — the arch, the knee, or the hip. That is the difference between flat feet that need treating and flat feet that do not.

In detail

Understandingflat feet

A flat or rolled-in foot is called over-pronation. Some rolling in is normal: it is how the foot absorbs shock. The problem is when it goes too far for too long.

How far should a foot roll in?

Heel upright

Ideal

Standing relaxed, the heel sits between upright and a few degrees tilted: 0–4°.

Up to 4°

Normal shock absorption

When walking, the foot should only roll in about 4° from its neutral position to absorb impact.

Beyond that

Over-pronation

The arch drops, the foot lengthens and the strain moves up the leg.

01

When the foundation drops

The foot is the body’s foundation: when it collapses, it brings everything above with it. A common pattern is inner heel pain in the morning, inner shin pain and inner knee pain together.

02

It runs in families

These biomechanical traits are often inherited and passed down through generations, often alongside a twist in the shin (pigeon-toed or out-toed).

03

Checking an orthotic

A support that flattens like a pancake under thumb pressure is not supporting much. An orthotic should also sit stable on a flat surface without wobbling.

04

Matched to you

How firm the orthotic needs to be depends on how far your foot rolls in and on your body weight. Different shoes may need different styles.
