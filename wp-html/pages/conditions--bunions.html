What you notice

Symptomsand causes

If this sounds like your pain, there is a structural reason for it. The assessment is what finds it.

Common symptoms

  • A bump on the inside of the foot at the base of the big toe
  • The big toe drifting across towards the second toe
  • Redness or rubbing where the joint presses on the shoe
  • Aching at the joint when walking or pushing off
  • Sometimes on one foot only

What causes it

The first of the long bones behind the big toe is often naturally a little short, and sits angled in towards the middle of the body. For it to reach the ground, the foot rolls in. As it does, the ground pushes the big toe across and rotates it, and over time the muscles that pull the toe inwards shorten and hold it there. Where a bunion forms on one side only, a longer leg rolling in more than the other is often behind it.

How we diagnose it

The cause,not the sore spot

Bunions is diagnosed with the Najjarine Biomechanical Assessment, so treatment is prescribed against a finding rather than a symptom.

01

Assess

We assess the foot as it loads, and measure the position of the first metatarsal and the big toe, rather than looking at the bump alone.

02

Diagnose

We identify what is driving the toe across — foot alignment, a short first metatarsal, a leg length difference, or footwear — and explain what we found.

03

Treat

Treatment is aimed at that cause, because a bunion that is not addressed mechanically tends to keep progressing through its stages.
